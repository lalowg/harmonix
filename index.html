ch.02signal pathA3 · 220.00 Hz

from a press to a picture

Nothing here is recorded. Each note is built from scratch with the Web Audio API, then read back many times a second and drawn. Six stages, start to finish.

  1. 01

    press

    A pad, a key, a finger.

    Each press sends a pitch and a moment. Nothing is sampled or pre-recorded: every note on this page is synthesised the instant you ask for it, then let go.

    pointer + keyboard · up to 12 voices

  2. 02

    oscillate

    Two oscillators, seven cents apart.

    The pair drift in and out of phase, which gives even a plain sine a slow shimmer. Choose sine, triangle, saw or square — the field changes texture with the harmonics.

    2 oscillators per voice · detune +7¢

  3. 03

    filter

    A low-pass that breathes.

    The filter opens wide on the attack and settles within a fraction of a second. The Tone fader decides where it settles: dark and woody, or bright and glassy.

    low-pass · 220 Hz – 9 kHz · Q 5

  4. 04

    shape

    Attack, decay, hold, release.

    An eight-millisecond attack keeps it percussive, a short decay softens the strike, and the note sustains for as long as you hold. Release sets how long it takes to fade once you let go.

    attack 8 ms · release 0.12 – 5 s

  5. 05

    space

    A room that doesn’t exist.

    The reverb is a 3.4-second impulse generated from shaped noise when audio starts; the echo is a filtered delay that feeds back into itself. Space and Echo set how much of each you hear.

    convolution 3.4 s · echo 360 ms

  6. 06

    see

    Read back, drawn forward.

    An analyser samples the output 2,048 points at a time and the canvas draws it: the waveform ripples through the field, the interval draws its figure, and the pitch class picks the colour.

    fft 2048 · canvas 2D · every frame

ch.03intervalsE4 · 329.63 Hz

every interval has a shape

Send two notes to the x and y inputs of an oscilloscope and their ratio draws itself. Simple ratios close into simple figures; complicated ones knot. Choose a pair to hear it and watch it.

ratiointervalspanET driftfigure

ET drift is how far equal temperament (the tuning of a piano, and of this instrument) sits from the whole-number ratio, in cents.

D 293.66 Hz hue 60°

ch.04pitch colourG4 · 392.00 Hz

every pitch has a colour

HARMONIX colours notes around the circle of fifths, not along the keyboard. Notes a fifth apart sit side by side on the wheel, so consonant chords read as neighbouring hues and tension reads as contrast.

Lit on the wheel

D major pentatonic

D · E · F# · A · B

A design mapping, not a claim about perception. Synaesthetes disagree with each other, and with us.
